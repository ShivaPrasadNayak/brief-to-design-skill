# Quality bar — what "good" looks like

Reference: a 9-slide brand-naming pitch deck for an AI productivity app ("DaySutra", a student project, 2026),
studied slide by slide. This is the bar every deliverable must meet or beat.
It is a reference for **craft**, not a template — don't reuse its blue palette or layouts for other subjects.

## What it does well (keep doing these)

1. **Headlines are claims, not topics.** "63 names tested. One clear winner." · "One brand, four touchpoints" ·
   "Six ways to get DaySutra found". A reader skimming titles gets the whole argument.
2. **Eyebrow + headline title block.** Small tracked uppercase label in the primary colour names the section
   ("HOW WE CHOSE", "BRAND ARCHITECTURE"); the large headline carries the point. Consistent position on every slide.
3. **The name's meaning drives the visual system.** *Sutra* = thread → the cover's three interweaving lines,
   a logo of a tangled thread ending in a straight line ("untangled"), the tagline "The thread that holds your
   day together." One metaphor, used everywhere, never explained twice.
4. **Dark/light sandwich with purpose.** Dark navy for the opening, the recommendation and the go-to-market
   slide (the moments that should feel like a stage); cool off-white for analysis slides.
5. **A named token palette with roles.** navy-900 #0A1630, sutra-blue #2F6BFF, blue-ink #1D4ED8, sky-300 #8DB8FF,
   thread-teal #22C3D6, ice-50 #F3F6FB — each with a job ("accents on dark", "highlights, progress").
   Type: Sora 600–700 display + DM Sans 400–700 body; scale 112/72/44/32/24; 24px radius; 4.5:1 body contrast.
6. **Numbers as heroes.** 3+, 5, 63 → 6 → 1 funnel. Big display numerals with a one-line label.
7. **Real data visualisation, highlight the answer.** Ranked bars: the winner in full primary with "27 · winner",
   the others muted. The chart says the conclusion without a caption.
8. **Show the product, don't describe it.** A browser-frame landing-page mockup with real copy and a phone-card
   UI ("Chai Plan · Tue", "2 of 3 done") made the brand tangible.
9. **Layout variety inside one system.** Hero · quote + stat stack · funnel + bars · statement + 2×2 · three-column
   spec · mockup · four-card row + banner quote · 3×2 grid · two lists + dark action panel. No two adjacent alike.
10. **Honest labelling.** "(persona model)", "Watch-outs: …", "All creator posts carry #ad … (ASCI)",
    availability checked "(Oct 2026)". Claims carry their basis.
11. **Ends on action.** Numbered next steps with owners-able verbs ("Buy daysutra.com this week").
12. **Quiet consistent chrome.** Footer "DaySutra · Team" left, page number right, small and muted.

## Where it falls short (do better)

1. **Every slide is a flat picture.** No live text: not editable, not searchable, not accessible, can't be fixed
   in PowerPoint. → Build PPTX with native text, shapes and charts. Images only for photos/illustrations.
2. **Dead space in a few frames.** Lower third of the mockup slide and the bottoms of the keyword columns are
   empty. → Size containers to content; let the composition breathe on purpose, not by leftover.
3. **Card-heavy.** 6 of 9 slides are rounded-card grids. → Reach for open compositions too: full-bleed
   image, oversized type, split screen, annotated diagram, a single statement slide.
4. **Edge-stripe accent on the GTM cards.** The thin top highlight line is the "AI-slide" tell. → Use tint,
   an icon, or a number instead.
5. **No photography.** Fine for a naming pitch, but the system had no image direction at all. → Every design
   system states its image direction, even if the answer is "none: type and diagrams only, because …".

## Bar checklist (derived)
- [ ] Skim test: reading only the headlines tells the full story.
- [ ] One metaphor from the subject's meaning, carried through cover, motif and one signature slide.
- [ ] Named tokens with roles; nothing off-system.
- [ ] At least one real data visual or product mockup where the content allows it.
- [ ] Layout variety: no two adjacent slides share a composition; cards ≤ half the slides.
- [ ] Claims sourced or labelled as model/assumption.
- [ ] Final slide/section is an action.
- [ ] Native, editable text in office formats.
